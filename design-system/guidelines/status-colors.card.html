Ships today — four tokens, both modes

success online, passed --success
warning needs attention --warning
info informational --info
destructive failed --destructive
Each token holds the colour itself, not a pale tint of it — so text-warning is the colour, bg-warning/5 is a faint wash of it, and bg-warning with text-warning-foreground is a solid fill. The -foreground is the neutral that goes on top, and it flips with the mode because the dark values are light and the light values are dark.

Both modes are defined, so a dark: variant on a status colour is a sign someone hand-rolled a palette value instead. Changed 2026-08-19 — see DD-012, which is also why bg-warning/5 is visible at all.

approval awaiting a human yes --approval

Approval is a fifth status, not a shade of warning. Blocked and awaiting approval are triaged differently and must be separable across a room. Hue 310 keeps it clear of info (255) and of the Violet brand preset (285).
Named rule — Identity Is Not Status. No actor-identity hue sits within 20° of a status hue. The six shipping hues are 50 / 135 / 185 / 235 / 285 / 335 — also the widest mutual separation available, 50°, because telling two agents apart is the one job this palette has. Identity is never themed: if a user accent could recolour it, two agents could collapse to one hue.
The avatar is a neutral chip with a coloured mark, not a coloured chip. §2.5 originally specified an identity tint plus the colour’s own foreground. Measured, that reaches 3.91:1 in dark mode — a 15% tint lifts the ground by more than the mark gains. A neutral fill with the identity colour on the mark and a 40% ring keeps both signals at 7.16 dark and 4.72 light. DD-013.
